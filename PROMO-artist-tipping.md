## ⚠️ Compliance note — READ THIS, it is what keeps the feature legal

The pitch above is built to be true and legal. It works because of the **leaderboard and the
100% pass-through**, not because of a tax claim. Keep it that way.

**Do NOT advertise "tax-free tips" anywhere a fan can see it.** It isn't accurate, and putting
it in fan-facing marketing is the fastest way to turn a good feature into a legal problem.

Here is the accurate version of the tax point, which you *may* say to **artists** (never as a
headline, always with the disclaimer):

- The 2026 federal "No Tax on Tips" rule created a **deduction** on qualified tips, up to
  **$25,000 a year**, and the IRS occupation list **does include musicians, singers and digital
  content creators.**
- But it is a **deduction against federal income tax for the person receiving the tip.** It is
  **not** a blanket exemption. It does **not** remove self-employment tax. It **phases out** at
  higher incomes. And it does **nothing** for the fan who sends the tip.
- So the honest line to an artist is: *"Your tips may qualify for the new federal tips
  deduction — talk to your accountant."* That's it. Nothing stronger.

**This is not tax advice.** Before any tax language goes on the live site or into an ad, have an
accountant approve the exact wording. On the public site, the wallet math is the promise: money
in, money to the artist, minus only the card fee.

*(The platform must also handle the boring-but-mandatory parts: issuing 1099s to artists who
cross the reporting threshold, and — because tips are income to the artist — not implying
otherwise. Stripe Connect does the 1099 filing automatically; see the payments section of the
README.)*
